A desktop that naps.

Loaf is a quiet desktop for Linux laptops. Nothing moves when you are not looking, so the battery goes to your work instead of the desktop.

Calm when idle, lighter at work.

Same laptop, same evening, same screen brightness, measured from what the battery itself reports. Doing nothing, Loaf and GNOME are equal. Once something moves on screen, niri does less than half of GNOME's compositing work, and that shows.

A Framework 13 (AMD Ryzen AI 300), 57 Wh battery, both desktops set up the same way. The in-use work is a script, so both got exactly the same. The method and the raw numbers are in the repository, under measurements.

One bar, and nothing else running.

Everything you need from a desktop lives in a single strip along the top. It only redraws when something changes: a new minute, a new notification, a few percent of battery.

5.1 W · 7h 40m 72%

The cat opens everythingApps, settings and files in one search. Super+D does the same.

The clock keeps your notificationsMissed something while you were away? It is under the time, with a dot until you look.

The right side is your laptopWi-Fi, watts, time left and battery. Click it for quick settings and the power menu.

When you stop, it stops.

The clockticks once a minute, not once a second.

Wi-Fionly looks for networks while the list of networks is open.

Notificationswait quietly until an app sends one. Nothing checks in the background.

The lock screentakes your finger or your password, and lets the laptop sleep at about 0.65 W: one percent an hour.

Light and darkfollow your system setting. Your apps, the login screen and the boot screen come along.

Try it next to what you have.

Loaf is a second login choice next to GNOME. If it is not for you, log out and pick GNOME again; nothing of yours changes.
